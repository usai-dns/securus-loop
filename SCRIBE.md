Sam,

This is a brilliant concept, and I think you're onto something huge here. Scribe could fill a real gap in the market - there's clearly demand for capturing personal stories, but the existing solutions are either too basic or too complicated for most people.

**What Makes This Different:**

The autonomous interviewing aspect is the killer feature. StoryWorth sends weekly prompts via email, but it's passive - they're not following up on your answers or diving deeper into interesting threads. LifeBio has some interview tools, but they're more structured questionnaires than dynamic conversations. What you're describing is more like having a skilled journalist or oral historian available 24/7, but one that learns your communication style and adapts in real-time.

Replika does conversational AI well, but it's focused on companionship and emotional support, not systematic story capture. Your Scribe would be purpose-built for extraction and documentation rather than relationship building.

**Technical Approach - How I'd Build This:**

**Phase 1 - Core Engine:**
Start with proven interview methodologies as your foundation. Look into life review techniques used by therapists, oral history best practices from institutions like StoryCorps, and narrative therapy approaches. These give you tested frameworks for helping people access and organize their memories.

For the AI interviewing engine, I'd build on existing conversational AI platforms rather than starting from scratch. OpenAI's API or similar could handle the natural language processing, but you'd need to train it specifically on interview techniques - active listening, follow-up questions, gentle probing, knowing when to change topics.

**Phase 2 - Voice and Audio Handling:**
Your voice synthesis approach is smart - capturing speech patterns without storing full audio files reduces costs and privacy concerns while still preserving personality. You'd need to identify the key vocal markers that make someone sound like themselves: cadence, pitch patterns, word choice, pause habits, emotional inflections.

The speaker differentiation feature is crucial and technically challenging. You'll need real-time voice fingerprinting to distinguish between the primary subject and family members jumping in. This exists in current tech but would need integration with your interview flow.

**Phase 3 - Integration and Output:**
The print-on-demand book connection is genius for monetization. Partner with existing services rather than building your own - IngramSpark, KDP, or Blurb for physical books. You could also offer digital formats, podcast-style audio stories, or even video documentaries using AI avatars.

**Key Questions Still to Answer:**

**Business Model:** How do you price this? Subscription for ongoing interviews? One-time fee per story project? Tiered pricing based on output formats?

**Data Retention:** How long do you keep the raw interview data vs. the processed stories? Some customers might want their data deleted after getting their book, others might want ongoing access.

**Quality Control:** How do you ensure the AI doesn't miss important stories or misinterpret emotional context? Maybe human oversight for complex interviews?

**Scope Management:** How do you prevent interviews from going on forever? Some people could talk for hundreds of hours - you need ways to help them focus and conclude their stories.

**Cultural Sensitivity:** Different cultures have different storytelling traditions and comfort levels with sharing personal information. How does Scribe adapt to these differences?

**Legal Considerations:** What happens if someone reveals criminal activity or family secrets during an interview? What are your obligations regarding mandatory reporting or privacy protection?

**Technical Infrastructure Questions:**

**Scalability:** How do you handle peak usage during holidays when families gather? Voice processing is computationally expensive.

**Security Architecture:** End-to-end encryption? On-device processing vs. cloud? How do you balance security with the need to improve your AI models?

**Backup and Disaster Recovery:** These are irreplaceable family stories - what happens if data is lost?

**Competitive Positioning:**

You're right that security will be a major concern. This is deeply personal information - more intimate than most social media data. Your competitive advantage could be positioning as the "privacy-first" story capture service. Maybe even offer fully local processing for ultra-security-conscious customers.

The adaptability angle is also strong. StoryWorth is one-size-fits-all prompts. Your Scribe could adapt to whether someone is naturally talkative or needs gentle encouragement, whether they prefer chronological storytelling or thematic approaches, whether they want to focus on factual events or emotional experiences.

**Corporate Knowledge Capture:** Retiring executives or key employees documenting institutional knowledge before they leave.

**Therapeutic Applications:** Working with therapists who want to help clients process life experiences (obviously requires careful HIPAA compliance).
