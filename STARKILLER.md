- What exactly are they? Theories range from cursed humans to former Amyr to something else entirely

**The Amyr Conspiracy:**
- Are they still active? Most fans think yes, working in secret
- Is the University connected to them? Kilvin and others seem suspicious
- What's their real relationship to the Chandrian - enemies, or two sides of the same coin?

- Who is her patron "Master Ash"? Leading theory is Cinder (one of the Chandrian), but others suggest Amyr connections

Sam,

This is a fascinating comparison to dig into - the strategic depth of Go actually maps onto Kingkiller in some really compelling ways that I think reveal a lot about how Rothfuss constructed his story.

**The Long Game vs. Immediate Tactics:**
Go is all about playing for position rather than immediate capture, and Kvothe's story follows this perfectly. His real enemies - the Chandrian, the Amyr, whatever forces killed his parents - require decades-long strategies to defeat. But Kvothe consistently gets pulled into short-term tactical thinking. He focuses on winning the immediate conversation, the current challenge, the present moment, often at the cost of his longer strategic position. His confrontation with Ambrose, his time in Tarbean, even his approach to learning at the University - he's constantly sacrificing strategic advantage for tactical victories.

The masters at the University, particularly Kilvin and Lorren, seem to understand the longer game in ways Kvothe doesn't. They're placing stones (knowledge, connections, resources) that won't pay off for years or decades. Kvothe wants immediate results.

In Go, controlling territory isn't about occupying every space - it's about establishing influence that makes certain areas effectively yours.
